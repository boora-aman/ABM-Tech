Three claims will get a client's listing suspended and your page reported.
Every post below is written to avoid them — keep it that way if you edit:

1. **Never say you bypass, skip or guarantee Google verification.**

   *handling the process end-to-end* — preparing the profile so it passes first
   time, submitting correctly, and dealing with rejections and reinstatements.
   Say **"we handle verification end to end"**, never *"instant verification"*.

2. **Never guarantee a ranking position.** Maps results are personalised by how
   far the searcher is standing from the business, so no single "position 1"
   exists to sell. Promise the work, the baseline and the monthly report.

3. **Never offer or imply bought reviews.** Fastest known route to a suspended
   profile, and it is reportable.

**On the word "instant":** use it for what you actually control — *"profile
built and submitted the same day"*. Do not use it for going live, because
that's Google's clock, not yours. Post 3 turns this into a selling point
instead of a limitation.

**₹2,500 = the one-time setup.** Ongoing optimisation is the ₹5,000/month
retainer. Keep the two clearly separate in every post or you will be doing

## Post 5 — Category engineering

> **Hook:** One field on your Google profile matters more than all the others. Most people pick it wrong.

**Caption:**
Your **primary category**.

It's the field Google uses to decide which searches you're even eligible to appear for. Get it wrong and no amount of reviews, photos or posts will fix it — you're competing in the wrong race.

**The mistake:** picking the category that describes what you *are*.
**The fix:** picking the one that matches what customers *search*.

Examples of the gap:
→ "Ayurvedic clinic" vs **"Ayurveda practitioner"** vs **"Alternative medicine practitioner"**
→ "Computer shop" vs **"Computer repair service"** vs **"Computer store"**
→ "Builder" vs **"General contractor"** vs **"Home builder"**
→ "Tiffin service" vs **"Meal delivery"** vs **"Caterer"**

Each pulls a completely different set of searches, with completely different competition.

**How to actually choose it:** search your main keyword, open the top three listings in the map pack, and check what category *they* use. That's reverse-engineering what Google has already decided works for that search.

Then set up to 9 secondary categories. Almost nobody does, and it's free.

## Post 8 — NAP consistency

> **Hook:** Your phone number is different in four places online. Google noticed.

**Caption:**
NAP = **Name, Address, Phone.** The rule is simple and almost universally broken: it must be **byte-identical everywhere.**

Not similar. Identical.

**Where yours probably differs right now:**
→ Google Business Profile
→ Your website footer
→ Your website's structured data (the part nobody checks)
→ JustDial, IndiaMART, Sulekha
→ Your Facebook page
→ Old directory entries from a vendor you used in 2019

**And these count as different:**
`Shop 4, MG Road` ≠ `Shop No. 4, M.G. Road`
`+91 91197 56710` ≠ `9119756710`
`ABM Tech` ≠ `ABM Tech Solutions`

Mismatched details don't merely fail to help. Google treats conflicting information as a signal that the business data is unreliable, and **actively suppresses the listing**. It's one of the most common reasons a good business ranks below a worse one.

A citation sweep is unglamorous, boring work, and it's often the single change that moves a listing.
